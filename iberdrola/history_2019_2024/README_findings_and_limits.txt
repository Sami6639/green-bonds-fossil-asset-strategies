Iberdrola, 2019–2024: capacity-led transition accounting

Main result
The published total of gas-fired capacity (under the issuer's 'Gas combined cycles' heading), cogeneration and coal was 16,864 MW in 2019,17,012 MW in 2020,17,006 MW in 2021,17,520 MW in 2022 and 2023, and 9,014 MW in 2024. These figures consistently combine the consolidated-at-EBITDA column with the proportional share managed by investees. They describe the reported corporate portfolio, not a fixed set of physical assets or pure equity-net ownership. The exact component values and provenance are in primary_capacity_summary.csv and capacity_by_fossil_technology_2019_2024.csv.

The observed 2019–2024 contraction of 7,850 MW decomposes into the following verified classified contributions:874 MW physical coal closures;8,494 MW Mexican gas-capacity divestment;779 MW completed Topolobampo III installation;514 MW commissioned Tamazunchale II;123 MW existing Smithfield OCGT acquired through Infigen;120 MW existing South Australian Gas Turbines acquired/operating-rights addition; and 17 MW Termopernambuco uprating. A further 35 MW of cogeneration reduction remains unresolved by mechanism. The 144 MW Enertek transfer between cogeneration and gas categories cancels in the combined total. This is a net-change reconciliation, not proof that all gross offsetting transactions have been discovered.

The evidence separates genuine retirement from ownership exit. Velilla 516 MW and Lada 358 MW were physically closed in 2020. The Mexican transaction completed 26 February 2024 and removed 8,494 MW from the reported gas portfolio through sale, not closure. On the evidence collected, sales account for the much larger verified exit. The aggregate portfolio was still larger in 2023 than in 2019, despite the coal closures.

Prior strategy evidence
The 10 November 2017 issuer press release explicitly sought closure authorisation for Lada and Velilla,874 MW in total. This decision predates the February 2019 matched green hybrid studied here, but does not predate Iberdrola's entire green-financing programme, which started 2014. Annual 2023 Note 18 separately documents a Mexico divestment MoU inApril 2023 and sale agreement inJune 2023, before 2024 completion. These sequences show why financing timing should not be equated with the origination of transition strategy. They do not identify what caused a strategy or whether finance affected execution.

Matched green-finance delivery evidence
A stable two-asset February 2019 hybrid, ISIN XS1890845875, is traceable in all six official allocation reports. It repeatedly reports EUR 800 m allocated and 231 MW attributable capacity, covering East Anglia 1(180 MW) and Wikinger(51 MW). Attributable generation is 232,761,874,821,948 and 771 GWh in 2019–2024. These are annual reported returns on a recurring allocation stock, not six annual financing flows and not additional generation caused by the bond. Per-project money and generation are not disclosed; finance/refinance percentages are not supplied by the selected six instrument tables and remain missing.
The green reports change East Anglia's startup year from 2019 in 2019–2021 reports to 2020 in 2022–2024. Factbook 2021 PDF 91 separately identifies operational East Anglia ONE with COD 2020 and full-project 714 MW, and operational Wikinger with COD 2017/full-project 350 MW. Thus the delivery case supports distinguishing financing attribution, first-generation/startup labels and operational COD. It does not independently verify incremental financing or causal fossil displacement. See matched_green_hybrid_2019_2024.csv, matched_green_hybrid_projects_2019_2024.csv and matched_project_delivery_evidence.csv.

Measurement gates
1. The primary scope is the published group total. A 204 MW gas capacity item moves from consolidated to proportional investees between 2022 and 2023 report vintages. This is not a disposal and leaves total capacity unchanged. capacity_reporting_revision.csv preserves both versions.
2. Coal 2020 zero is directly represented by dashes in the exhaustive capacity table. Coal 2021–2024 is absent as a row: zeros are explicitly marked derived_structural_zero, supported by documented complete coal exit and subsequent exhaustive technology tables. They are not disguised as observed cells.
3. The gas category includes OCGT, as the 2021 factbook directly shows. Use reported gas-fired capacity or preserve the exact issuer label; do not claim uniform combined-cycle engineering.
4. Cogeneration is not independently verified as a homogeneous fossil-fuel category. The combined gas/cogeneration/coal total is a covered technology proxy. The earlier pilot also identified a fuel-cell ambiguity within the reported Batteries production category. Do not label the proxy an exhaustive fossil-fuel inventory.
5. Capacity may include completed but not operating assets. Topolobampo is the documented example. Separate installed capacity, operating capacity, closure, sale, commissioning and expected startup.
6. Mexican headline deal capacity 8,539 MW includes wind and differs by 58 MW from operating-table gas plus wind capacity movements(8,494+103). The primary bridge uses the internally coherent operational series; the headline discrepancy remains unresolved.
7. The green-financing reports exclude separately issued Avangrid and Neoenergia instruments while the operating scope includes those businesses. Selected holding-level instruments cannot measure total-group green financing.
8. This is one issuer and six annual observations. No pooled fossil-GWh regression, causal design or inferential significance test has been attempted.

Permitted conclusion
The Iberdrola case indicates that retirement, acquisition, reclassification and divestment can produce materially different interpretations of apparently similar corporate capacity changes, while project-level green-finance returns remain conditional on allocation and reporting conventions. It establishes a source-backed accounting distinction and descriptive chronology; it does not establish the causal effect of green financing on physical decarbonisation.

2024 issuance-cohort distinction
The separate pilot's 8-ISIN cohort is selected by 2024 issue date. Its disclosed investment-assignment amounts sum toEUR 3,660 m across instrument pages, but this sum must not be called 2024 realised allocation stock, financing flow or spending: theEUR 800 m November hybrid XS2949317676 explicitly assigns the existing assets from 7 February 2025. Report year, issuance year, assignment effective date, spending date and project operating date are distinct. The six-year 2019 hybrid case here is a different instrument, XS1890845875; its EUR 800 m recurring assignment and returns remain separately recorded through 2024. Neither repeated annual reports nor the later replacement hybrid create additional physical capacity.
