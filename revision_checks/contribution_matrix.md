Contribution boundaries and closest predecessors

Alova (2020)
- Already establishes: utility portfolio transitions and concurrent renewable/fossil expansion across more than 3,000 utilities.
- Remaining question addressed here: what particular financial observation accompanies a dated asset change, and which finer allocation or implementation relationship can actually be observed?
- Added connection: dated financing states, ownership-sensitive asset windows and selected instrument histories.
- Importance: a portfolio direction alone does not identify allocation composition, project delivery or physical cessation.
- Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41560-020-00686-5

Tuhkanen and Vulturius (2022)
- Already establishes: links between corporate climate targets, financing frameworks and reporting; refinancing, allocation detail and co-ownership attribution problems. The sample includes 20 European issuers,11 in energy.
- Remaining question addressed here: how do recurring project membership, allocation weights and achieved milestones change between identified reporting dates?
- Added connection: reproducible within-instrument histories that separate amounts, membership, monetary composition and implementation.
- Importance: stable totals or recurring project names cannot alone establish an unchanged allocation composition, and continuing allocation can accompany later delivery.
- Source: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/20430795.2020.1857634

Bruegel and Leung (2024), IEEFA
- Already establishes: financing/reporting quality and transition progress among 12 European utilities.
- Remaining question addressed here: the chronological relation between fixed/changing allocation, achieved delivery and ownership-related changes to the eligible pool.
- Added connection: explicit dated contrasts and a distinction between documented links, coexistence and unresolved evidence.
- Importance: a broad green-finance assessment does not establish every instrument-to-asset relationship.
- Source: https://ieefa.org/resources/europes-clean-power-leaders-how-green-financing-enabling-renewables-growth

Defensible common contribution

The study complements these predecessors through a dated, ownership-sensitive account of relationships among financial commitment, allocation composition and achieved implementation. The broad portfolio comparison supplies context; qualified instrument histories support finer claims about project membership, monetary weights, operational stages and allocation replacement. Corporate fossil ownership remains a separately matched level unless documents establish a specific connection. These are complementary contributions, not proof that no previous study examined an individual component.

Signaling boundary

Spence and Connelly motivate the distinction between an observable financing action and the underlying attribute inferred from it. The present archive distinguishes maintenance of eligible uses, project delivery and productive-asset change; it does not test differential signaling costs, investor updating, separating equilibrium or organizational capabilities. Connelly's requirements therefore constrain, rather than license expansion of, the manuscript's theoretical claims.

Sources: https://academic.oup.com/qje/article-abstract/87/3/355/1909092;https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/01492063241268459

Excluded claims: first study; representative global prevalence; financing additionality; causal green-bond effects; investor responses; company capability identification; and system-wide emissions reductions inferred from ownership exits.
